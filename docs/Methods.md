# Methodology & Robustness Audit

This document describes the data construction, variable transformations, three-stage variable screening and Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE) estimation procedures, variance decomposition formulas, and diagnostic audits underlying the municipal housing price analysis for the **Lisbon (AML)** and **Porto (AMP)** Metropolitan Areas (`2009–2026`).

---

## 1. Study Design, Panel Coverage & 2026 Data Cut-Off

### 1.1 Cross-Sectional & Temporal Coverage
- **Observational unit:** Municipality ($i = 1, \dots, G$) $\times$ Calendar Year ($t \in \{2009, \dots, 2026\}$), spanning **35 metropolitan municipalities**:
  - **Porto Metropolitan Area (`AMP`, $G = 17$):** NUTS III `11A` (*Arouca, Espinho, Gondomar, Maia, Matosinhos, Oliveira de Azeméis, Paredes, Porto, Póvoa de Varzim, Santa Maria da Feira, Santo Tirso, São João da Madeira, Trofa, Vale de Cambra, Valongo, Vila do Conde, Vila Nova de Gaia*).
  - **Lisbon Metropolitan Area (`AML`, $G = 18$):** Harmonised 18-municipality metropolitan boundary combining *Grande Lisboa* (9 municipalities north of the Tagus) and *Península de Setúbal* (9 municipalities south of the Tagus) to maintain longitudinal consistency across the 2024 NUTS III revision (*Alcochete, Almada, Amadora, Barreiro, Cascais, Lisboa, Loures, Mafra, Moita, Montijo, Odivelas, Oeiras, Palmela, Seixal, Sesimbra, Setúbal, Sintra, Vila Franca de Xira*).
- **Estimation scopes & typologies (12 Stage 3 models in total):**
  - **Real Bank Appraisals (`9` models):** 3 dwelling typologies (`Total`, `Apartments`, `Houses`) $\times$ 3 geographic scopes (`ALL`, `AMP`, `AML`), estimated over observed years **2011–2026**.
  - **Real Dwelling Sales (`3` models):** `Total` dwellings $\times$ 3 geographic scopes (`ALL`, `AMP`, `AML`). Only the aggregate `Total` category is estimated because municipal typology splits (`Apartments`, `Houses`) are not populated in the extracted INE transaction-price table (`varcd=0012239`). Moreover, although the estimation code sets an admissibility floor of `2016+` (reflected in dashboard dropdown labels), INE publishes this municipal series starting in **2019**, yielding an effective estimation window of **2019–2026** ($N = 279$ across $G = 35$ municipalities in `ALL`, with one confidentially suppressed cell).
  - Each of the 12 models reaches the full ceiling of **7 municipal predictors plus 1 interest-rate exposure interaction ($K = 8$ in every specification, verified across all 12 models)**, producing **96 estimated slope coefficients** overall (excluding absorbed municipality and year fixed effects).

### 1.2 2026 Data Cut-Off, Sub-Annual Aggregation & Hybrid HICP/CPI Deflator
The dataset reflects official statistical releases harvested through **November 2026**:
- **Sub-annual outcome & interest-rate aggregation (`Q1–Q4` historical vs. `Q1–Q3` 2026):** Quarterly INE municipal bank appraisals (`varcd=0012248`) and quarterly INE municipal dwelling sales medians (`varcd=0012239`) are aggregated to calendar-year observations using **one uniform rule across all 35 municipalities**: the **annual unweighted arithmetic mean of the available quarterly municipal median values**. In completed historical years (`2011–2025` for appraisals; `2019–2025` for sales), each municipality-year averages all **four quarters (`Q1–Q4`)** published by INE (if individual low-volume quarters in a smaller municipality are confidentially suppressed by INE, the annual figure averages the non-suppressed quarters in that year, and is missing only when all four quarters are suppressed); **2026 is provisional and based on `Q1–Q3`** (the quarters published up to the November 2026 cut-off, rather than a pooled full-year property-level median). Monthly Banco de Portugal / ECB Euribor and implicit mortgage interest rates are likewise aggregated as unweighted arithmetic means of available months within each calendar year.
- **RNAL stock vs. flow construction:** Each establishment in the National Registry of Local Accommodation (`RNAL / SIGTUR`, extracted November 2026) is assigned to the calendar year of its registration/opening date (`DataRegisto` / `DataAberturaPublico`):
  - `rnal_new_reg_total`: **annual flow** of new registrations opened in municipality $i$ during year $t$;
  - `rnal_density_per_km2`, `rnal_density_per_1k_hab`, `rnal_apt_per_1k_hab`: **cumulative gross stocks** of registrations opened up to year $t$ (including pre-2009 registrations, scaled by municipal area or resident population; deregistrations are not netted out because historical cancellation dates are unavailable in the open extract);
  - `rnal_moradias_per_1k_hab`: cumulative house-AL stock per 1,000 inhabitants, which enters models in **annual log-stock differences** ($\Delta \ln(1 + \text{stock}_{it})$) per the stationarity pre-screening rule in Section 2.2.
- **Publication lags in annual municipal covariates:** Official annual Pordata/INE municipal series end between **2022 and 2025** depending on release schedules:
  - Through **2025:** resident population, ageing index, net migration, employees in *Quadros de Pessoal*, completed dwellings, building permits, higher-education enrolment/graduates, physicians, crime rate, registered unemployment, and municipal IMI tax rates.
  - Through **2024:** average monthly earnings, non-financial enterprises, municipal capital expenditure, and IRS gross income per tax household (`2018–2024`).
  - Through **2023:** foreign resident population and biennially published purchasing-power index.
  - Through **2022:** classical family dwelling stock (`alojamentos_classicos_stock`).
- **Hybrid HICP/CPI chained deflator & provisional 2026 scale (`2026 = 100.0`):** Nominal prices $P^{\text{nom}}_{it,s}$ are converted to constant 2026 euros via $P^{\text{real}}_{it,s} = P^{\text{nom}}_{it,s} \times (100 / I_t)$, chained backward recursively from $I_{2026} = 100.000$ via $I_{t-1} = I_t / (1 + \pi_t / 100)$. Tracing the exact ingestion logic establishes that the cached deflator is **a hybrid Eurosystem HICP / national CPI rate-chained index** rather than a pure annual-average index-level series: when INE's annual-average CPI rate indicator (`varcd=0002373`) is queried without historical period-dimension metadata, the endpoint returns only the **single latest published annual CPI rate (`2025`: `2.20%`)**; because fewer than 12 historical years are returned, the harvester triggers its Eurosystem HICP fallback (`ECB Data Portal, ICP.M.PT.N.000000.4.ANR`), computing each calendar year's $\pi_t$ as the **12-month unweighted arithmetic mean of monthly HICP year-on-year percentage changes** and filling `2009–2024` via `setdefault` (preserving `2025 = 2.20%` from `INE 0002373`, while monthly INE `0002386` contributes no additional years). Finally, because full-year 2026 annual inflation was not yet published, **$\pi_{2026} = 2.20\%$ is an assumed carry-forward of the 2025 rate used to derive the 2025 index ($I_{2025}$) once the 2026 base index is fixed at $I_{2026} = 100.000$**.

  **Inflation-rate to index-year mapping:** Because the recursion is $I_{t-1} = I_t / (1 + \pi_t / 100)$ with $I_{2026} = 100.000$, the inflation rate for year $t$ determines the index for year $t - 1$, **not** $I_t$. The assumed 2026 carry-forward rate ($\pi_{2026}$) therefore determines $I_{2025}$, while the 2025 INE CPI rate ($\pi_{2025}$) determines $I_{2024}$, and so on back to $I_{2009}$ (determined by $\pi_{2010}$).   > **Methodological Compromises, Descriptive Conditionality & TWFE Invariance:** Two compromises in this cached deflator should be noted (and are scheduled for replacement in future releases; see Section 7): (i) for `2010–2024`, chaining the **12-month arithmetic mean of monthly year-on-year HICP inflation rates** (`ANR`) is a reproducible annual summary, but is not algebraically identical to the percentage change in an **annual-average HICP index level** (`INX`, which was not harvested locally) when intra-year inflation accelerates or decelerates; and (ii) retaining `INE 0002373` (`2.20%`) for `2025` ahead of Eurosystem HICP reflects an ingestion-order (`setdefault`) artefact rather than an ECB data-availability constraint, introducing a minor `2024 → 2025` HICP-to-national-CPI definitional splice alongside the provisional `2026` carry-forward (`2.20%`). Consequently, **all public-facing constant-2026 €/m² price levels and cumulative real percentage changes are provisional and conditional on this hybrid deflator**. By contrast — holding the estimation sample and covariate transformations fixed — **estimated TWFE slope coefficients, cluster-robust covariance estimates, $p$-values, $R^2_{\text{within}}$, and Shapley/LMG within-$R^2$ allocations are strictly invariant** to any common national deflator, since $\ln P^{\text{real}}_{it,s} = \ln P^{\text{nom}}_{it,s} - \ln(I_t / 100)$ adds a purely year-specific constant absorbed by year fixed effects $\lambda_t$.
- **Four distinct provisional mechanisms in `2026` observations:** The `2026` panel does not represent a uniformly observed, complete annual cross-section. Four types of data availability should be distinguished:
  1. **Housing outcomes (`appraisal_eur_m2`, `sales_eur_m2`):** partial-year observations based on the available `Q1–Q3 2026` quarterly municipal medians;
  2. **RNAL:** registrations and cumulative stocks extracted in November 2026. The `2026` registration flow is therefore incomplete and should be interpreted as registrations recorded up to the extraction date, not as a full-year total;
  3. **Interest rates and municipal covariates:** monthly interest-rate averages use the months available up to the November 2026 cut-off. Annual municipal covariates are observed through their latest published year; where the required lagged source-year value is unavailable, Layer 1 carries forward the last available value. Availability is determined by the predictor's **lagged source year**, not simply by the outcome year;
  4. **Price deflator:** the `2026` index is anchored at `100.000` using a provisional inflation-rate assumption of `2.20%` carried forward from `2025`. This assumption affects the backward construction of `2025` and earlier index values, not the `2026` base value itself.
  Consequently, `2026` observations combine partial-year outcomes, partial-year administrative and monthly data, carried-forward annual covariates, and a provisional deflator assumption. They should be identified as provisional throughout the analysis and dashboard.
- **Confidentiality suppressions in outcome series:** INE suppresses municipal medians in low-volume cells. Consequently, `ALL` bank appraisal samples over the 16-year window `2011–2026` ($35 \times 16 = 560$ potential cells) contain $N = 538$ (`Total`, $G = 35$), $N = 513$ (`Apartments`, $G = 34$), and $N = 442$ (`Houses`, $G = 33$).
- **Metropolitan averages vs. INE NUTS III medians:** Metropolitan price series are calculated as unweighted arithmetic means of the municipal indicators ($\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^G P_{it,s}$). They therefore differ from the corresponding INE NUTS III median indicators, which are calculated at a higher geographical aggregation level and need not equal the unweighted mean of municipal medians.

## 2. Notation, Transformations & Two-Layer Imputation (Stage 1)

### 2.1 Notation & Standardisation Conventions
| Symbol | Definition |
|---|---|
| $i \in \{1,\dots,G\},\; t \in \{1,\dots,T\}$ | Municipality index ($G$ clusters) and calendar year index ($T$ distinct years in estimation sample). |
| $N,\; K,\; K_{\text{abs}}$ | Total municipality-year observations ($N$), number of slope regressors ($K = 8$), and total absorbed parameters $K_{\text{abs}} = K + G + (T - 1)$ under a connected municipality–year incidence graph. |
| $y_{it} = \ln P^{\text{real}}_{it,s}$ | Natural logarithm of real price per $\text{m}^2$ (`2026 €`). |
| $\tilde{v}_{it}$ | Two-way demeaned vector after iterative alternating projection removing municipality and year means (tolerance $10^{-10}$). |
| $s_0(v)$ | Population-denominator standard deviation $s_0(v) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N (v_n - \bar{v})^2}$ (`ddof = 0`). |
| $\hat{\beta}_j,\; \hat{\beta}_{\text{std},j}$ | Unstandardised TWFE slope $\hat{\beta}_j$ and within-standardised slope $\hat{\beta}_{\text{std},j} = \hat{\beta}_j \cdot \frac{s_0(\tilde{x}_j)}{s_0(\tilde{y})}$. |
| $R^2_{\text{within}},\; R^2_{\text{overall}}$ | Within-municipality/year explained variance $1 - \frac{\sum \hat{e}_{it}^2}{\sum \tilde{y}_{it}^2}$ vs. full-fit explained variance $1 - \frac{\sum (y_{it} - \hat{\alpha}_i - \hat{\lambda}_t - x_{it}^\top\hat{\beta})^2}{\sum (y_{it} - \bar{y})^2}$. |

### 2.2 Candidate Catalogue, Exact Series Accounting & Stationarity Pre-Screening
1. **Exact series accounting (`73 → 47 → 39`):**
   - **Full catalogue (`73` series):** **63 municipal series** (`52` Pordata — comprising 21 base municipal indicators plus derived per-1,000-resident and per-$\text{km}^2$ scalings — `6` INE housing-supply indicators, and `5` RNAL indicators) plus **10 national `BPstat`/Eurosystem macro and regulatory series** (`63 + 10 = 73`).
   - **Analytical master-panel pruning (`47` series):** Removing **26 redundant municipal scale variants** (`12` unscaled raw counts where normalised rates exist, plus `14` duplicate `_per_km2` series while retaining `pop_densidade_km2` and `rnal_density_per_km2`) leaves **37 analytical municipal series + 10 national `BPstat` series = 47 analytical series** in the wide master panel (`73 - 26 = 47`).
   - **Stage 1 missingness eligibility (`39` predictors):** Series require $\le 35\%$ raw missingness over `2009–2026`, or $\le 70\%$ for official multi-year/biennial series spanning $\ge 5$ years with observations up to $\ge 2022$. Across the **63 catalogued municipal series**, **14 sparse series** (`missing_pct >= 94.4%`, spanning all scale variants of municipal IMT revenue, urban land share, domestic electricity, construction GVA, apartment/house completions split, urban transactions, and rehab permit share) fail eligibility and also have $< 6$ observations; **8 of those 14** sit among the 37 analytical municipal series in the wide panel. Dropping those 8 sparse series leaves **`37 - 8 = 29` eligible municipal predictors + `10` national `BPstat` series = `39` Stage 1 eligible predictors**.
   - **Stage 2 hypothesis count (`117` per family):** Expanding all 39 eligible predictors across **3 lags ($l \in \{0, 1, 2\}$)** yields **$39 \times 3 = 117$ candidate `(predictor, lag)` hypotheses** per `(dwelling_type, metro_scope)` (`1,053` screening regressions across all 9 appraisal families).
2. **Transformations & persistence pre-screening statistic:**
   - Across the 39 Stage 1 eligible predictors, functional transformations are assigned explicitly as follows:
     - **Non-negative, right-skewed count, stock, density, and per-1,000-inhabitant predictors (19 series)** receive $f(x) = \ln(1 + \max(0, x))$: `pop_residente`, `pop_densidade_km2`, `pop_estrangeira_per_1k_hab`, `alojamentos_classicos_stock_per_1k_hab`, `fogos_reabilitados_per_1k_hab`, `trabalhadores_conta_outrem_per_1k_hab`, `dormidas_turisticas_per_1k_hab`, `capacidade_alojamento_turistico_per_1k_hab`, `alunos_ensino_superior_per_1k_hab`, `medicos_por_1k_hab`, `taxa_criminalidade_1k`, `empresas_nao_financeiras_per_1k_hab`, `completed_dwellings_per_1k`, `building_permits_per_1k`, `rnal_density_per_1k_hab`, `rnal_apt_per_1k_hab`, `rnal_moradias_per_1k_hab` (subsequently first-differenced as $\Delta \ln(1 + x)$), `rnal_density_per_km2`, and `rnal_new_reg_total`.
     - **Strictly positive monetary predictors (5 series)** also receive $f(x) = \ln(1 + x)$ in the baseline pipeline: `ganho_medio_mensal` (`EUR`, $\min = 698.2$), `irs_rendimento_bruto_medio` (`EUR`, $\min = 8{,}971.0$), `new_housing_credit_eur_m` (`M EUR`, $\min = 1{,}935.1$), `despesa_capital_municipal_per_1k_hab` (`thousand EUR / 1k inhab.`, $\min = 4.11$), and `renda_mediana_m2` (`EUR/m²`, $\min = 3.32$). Because $\ln(1 + x)$ is unit-dependent for monetary variables, using $\ln(x)$ is preferable for strictly positive monetary series; empirically, the two-way demeaned correlation between $\ln(1 + x)$ and $\ln(x)$ is $> 0.999999$ for `ganho_medio_mensal` and `irs_rendimento_bruto_medio`, $0.9997$ for `despesa_capital_municipal_per_1k_hab`, and $0.9536$ for `renda_mediana_m2` (which enters only `Sales · Total · AML`). Direct model-level sensitivity testing of `Sales · Total · AML` ($N = 144$, $G = 18$) replacing $\ln(1 + x)$ with $\ln(x)$ for `renda_mediana_m2` (and identically when also switching `irs_rendimento_bruto_medio` to $\ln(x)$) confirms negligible impact: $R^2_{\text{within}}$ moves from `0.6094` to `0.6106`, `renda_mediana_m2` remains statistically insignificant ($\hat{\beta} = +0.3035 \to +0.2882$, $\hat{\beta}_{\text{std}} = +0.090 \to +0.102$, $p_{\text{CR2}} = 0.3189 \to 0.2624$, $p_{\text{WCB-Webb}} = 0.3715 \to 0.3060$, LMG `7.7% → 9.4%`), and both statistically supported drivers are virtually unchanged (`pop_estrangeira_per_1k_hab` ($t-1$): $\hat{\beta}_{\text{std}} = +0.534 \to +0.530$, $p_{\text{CR2}} = 0.0019$, $p_{\text{WCB-Webb}} = 0.0030$, LMG `46.5% → 45.6%`; `rnal_new_reg_total` ($t-2$): $\hat{\beta}_{\text{std}} = +0.233 \to +0.230$, $p_{\text{CR2}} = 0.0392 \to 0.0394$, $p_{\text{WCB-Webb}} = 0.0130 \to 0.0125$, LMG `14.3% → 14.0%`). Migrating strictly positive monetary predictors to $\ln(x)$ is listed in Section 7.
     - **Rates, percentages, indices, policy dummies, and net migration (15 series)** enter in levels: `saldo_migratorio_per_1k_hab`, `indice_envelhecimento`, `taxa_desemprego_registado`, `poder_compra_per_capita`, `diplomados_ensino_superior_pct`, `custom_taxa_imi_urbano_pct`, `euribor_3m_pct`, `euribor_6m_pct`, `euribor_12m_pct`, `mortgage_implicit_rate_pct`, `golden_visa_regime`, `rnh_regime_active`, `bdp_ltv_dsti_2018`, `al_containment_regime`, and `youth_imt_guarantee_2024`.
   - During catalogue construction, all 63 municipal series are checked for persistence: the **14 sparse municipal series** have $< 6$ non-null observations and are skipped, while the remaining **49 catalogued municipal series** (`63 - 14 = 49`, which include all 29 Stage 1 eligible municipal predictors plus 20 of their pruned scale variants) have $\ge 6$ non-null values and are demeaned prior to autoregression:
     - **Balanced calendar-year coverage vs. implementation check:** Formally, two-way demeaning by sequential year and municipality averaging requires **identical non-null calendar-year coverage** across all retained municipalities (equal non-null observation counts alone are not sufficient in general if municipalities span misaligned year subsets; when coverage is unbalanced, cross-sectional time demeaning should be skipped and only municipality means removed). Although the code's guard checks equal non-null counts (`len(lengths) == 1`) rather than explicit year-set equality, Layer 1 never back-fills prior to a series' initial observed year and carries forward uniformly to `2026` thereafter. Direct verification across all **49 evaluated municipal series** confirms **zero misaligned or unequal-length cases**: every evaluated series has **identical calendar-year coverage across all 35 municipalities** — specifically `2009–2026` ($T = 18$) for 43 series, `2011–2026` ($T = 16$) for 4 series (`completed_dwellings_per_1k` and the three `saldo_migratorio` scalings), `2018–2026` ($T = 9$) for `irs_rendimento_bruto_medio`, and `2020–2026` ($T = 7$) for `renda_mediana_m2`. Consequently, cross-sectional year demeaning followed by municipality demeaning is applied on an exactly aligned balanced grid for all 49 series.
   - A pooled autoregression without augmentation lags, $\Delta \tilde{x}_{it} = \rho\,\tilde{x}_{i,t-1} + u_{it}$, is then estimated by OLS, computing the **pooled screening ratio**:
     $$S_\rho = \frac{\hat{\rho}}{\sqrt{s^2_u / \sum_{i,t} \tilde{x}_{i,t-1}^2}}, \qquad s^2_u = \frac{\sum_{i,t} \hat{u}_{it}^2}{N_{\text{diff}} - G - 1}$$
     Series with **$S_\rho \ge -2.0$** are transformed into annual first differences ($\Delta f(x_{it}) = f(x_{it}) - f(x_{i,t-1})$).
   - **Heuristic & degrees-of-freedom invariance:** $S_\rho$ is a **pragmatic pre-screening statistic**, not a formal Student-$t$, Levin–Lin–Chu, or Im–Pesaran–Shin test. Although the implemented variance denominator $N_{\text{diff}} - G - 1$ omits the additional $T - 1$ rank absorbed by cross-sectional time demeaning, recomputing $S_\rho$ with exact two-way residual degrees of freedom **$N_{\text{diff}} - G - T$** yields **zero classification changes** (`0` flips across all 29 eligible municipal predictors at the $-2.0$ threshold). National `BPstat` series bypass this rule and enter in levels (or $\ln(1+x)$ for credit volume) interacted with $z_{i,\mathcal{G}}$.

### 2.3 Two-Layer Missing-Data Algorithm in the Baseline Pipeline
To avoid ambiguity between "non-null master-panel cells" and "raw, unimputed data", the baseline pipeline applies two distinct operations at different stages:

1. **Layer 1 — Cross-sectional median-increment imputation and carry-forward (during master-panel construction):**
   - Layer 1 is applied across the annual master panel ($t \in \{2009, \dots, 2026\}$) before lag construction. It uses observed cross-sectional annual increments to estimate missing municipality-year values while preserving each municipality's previous valid value as the starting point:
     - At $t = 2009$, missing municipality values are filled with the cross-sectional median of observed municipalities, $\hat{x}_{i,2009} = \text{median}_{j \in \mathcal{O}_{2009}}(x_{j,2009})$, where available. If *no* municipality is observed in 2009 (e.g. series starting in 2011, 2018, or 2020), `2009` remains `null`.
     - For $t > 2009$, let $\mathcal{O}_t$ denote the set of municipalities with raw observations in year $t$ and $\mathcal{O}_{t-1}^{\text{imp}}$ denote the set of municipalities with valid (raw or previously Layer 1-imputed) values in year $t - 1$. The cross-sectional median increment is calculated from municipalities with valid values in both year $t$ and year $t - 1$:
       $$\Delta_t^{\text{med}} = \begin{cases} \operatorname{median}_{j \in \mathcal{O}_t \cap \mathcal{O}_{t-1}^{\text{imp}}}\!\left(x_{j,t} - \hat{x}_{j,t-1}\right) & \text{if } \mathcal{O}_t \cap \mathcal{O}_{t-1}^{\text{imp}} \neq \emptyset, \\ 0 & \text{if } \mathcal{O}_t \cap \mathcal{O}_{t-1}^{\text{imp}} = \emptyset \end{cases}$$
       which sets $\Delta_t^{\text{med}} = 0$ both when no municipality is observed in year $t$ ($\mathcal{O}_t = \emptyset$, e.g. unpublished `2025–2026` annual covariates) and whenever no municipality has valid values in both $t$ and $t - 1$ (e.g. the initial observed year of a post-2009 series, where $\mathcal{O}_{t-1}^{\text{imp}} = \emptyset$). Missing values are then carried forward from the previous valid municipality-specific value, adjusted by the estimated median increment where one can be calculated: $\hat{x}_{i,t} = \hat{x}_{i,t-1} + \Delta_t^{\text{med}}$.
     - When normalising INE housing-supply counts per 1,000 inhabitants in `2026` (after observed `2025` population), the denominator carries forward the municipality's `2025` resident population.
   - This procedure does not backfill years preceding a series' first observed year. However, because it uses contemporaneous cross-sectional information from other municipalities when calculating annual increments, **it should not be interpreted as an exclusively backward-looking imputation procedure**.
2. **Layer 2 — Estimation-time neutral mean fill on the transformed scale (during design-matrix extraction):**
   - Cells that remain `null` in the Layer 1-processed master panel (unavailable initial lags $t-1, t-2$, initial first differences $\Delta$, or years prior to a post-2009 series' start year) are filled during multivariate design-matrix assembly — **prior to CV fold separation and Stability Selection subsampling** — **after applying the predictor's functional/stationarity transformation** ($\ln(1 + x)$, level, or annual difference $\Delta f(x)$). Specifically, $\bar{x}_i$ is computed on that **transformed regression scale** as municipality $i$'s arithmetic mean of non-null transformed Layer 1 values within the active estimation sample (`2011–2026` for appraisals; `2019–2026` for sales), falling back to the active sample's global transformed mean $\bar{x}$ only if municipality $i$ has zero non-null values over that window. Because municipality-specific means $\bar{x}_i$ depend exclusively on municipality $i$'s own observations, they do not pool information across municipalities under municipality-grouped folds or subsamples (for held-out validation municipalities in CV, $\bar{x}_i$ uses that municipality's full observed time series, consistent with the conditional-within-municipality scope in Section 3.2); by contrast, the global fallback mean $\bar{x}$ pools across all municipalities in the active sample before splitting.

> **Terminology clarification:** In Stage 2B univariate screening, regressions drop `null` master-panel cells (avoiding **Layer 2** mean-fill) but **still use series processed through Layer 1**. Conversely, the **raw-data (no-imputation) sensitivity analysis** in Section 5.5 bypasses **both Layer 1 and Layer 2**, rebuilding the panel directly from raw, unimputed agency files under strict listwise deletion.

## 3. High-Dimensional Variable Screening (Stage 2)

### 3.1 Continuous Urban-Density Exposure for National Series
Because national macroeconomic and regulatory series $M_t$ (`BPstat`) are invariant across municipalities and absorbed by year fixed effects $\lambda_t$, each national series is centred around its active-sample mean $\bar{M}_{\mathcal{G}}$ and interacted with a standardised time-invariant baseline urban-density exposure $z_{i,\mathcal{G}}$:
$$z_{i,\mathcal{G}} = \frac{\ln(\text{Pop}_{i, t_0} / \text{Area}_i) - \mu_{z,\mathcal{G}}}{s_{0,\mathcal{G}}(z)}, \qquad X^{\text{macro}}_{it,l} = z_{i,\mathcal{G}} \cdot \left(M_{t-l} - \bar{M}_{\mathcal{G}}\right)$$
where $t_0$ is the earliest observed population year (`2009`). In the current implementation, **$\mu_{z,\mathcal{G}}$ and $s_{0,\mathcal{G}}(z)$ (`ddof = 0`) are calculated separately within the set of observed municipalities $\mathcal{G}$ of each estimation sample** (`ALL` $G = 33\text{–}35$, `AMP` $G = 15\text{–}17$, `AML` $G = 18$), rather than once globally across all 35 municipalities. Because shifting $\mu_{z,\mathcal{G}}$ adds a purely time-varying term $\Delta\mu_z \cdot (M_{t-l} - \bar{M}_{\mathcal{G}})$ absorbed identically by year fixed effects $\lambda_t$ and dividing by $s_{0,\mathcal{G}}(z)$ is a positive constant rescaling within sample $\mathcal{G}$, **within-standardised slopes ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{std}}$), $t$-statistics, $p$-values, $R^2_{\text{within}}$, and Shapley/LMG shares are invariant** to scope-specific vs. global 35-municipality standardisation; however, **unstandardised interaction slopes ($\hat{\gamma}_s$) are expressed on scope-specific exposure scales and should not be compared directly in raw unstandardised units across `ALL`, `AMP`, and `AML`** (adopting a single 35-municipality reference scale is listed as a planned improvement in Section 7).

### 3.2 Grouped Cross-Validation, One-Step Validation Projection & Stability Selection (Stage 2A)
All 117 `(predictor, lag)` columns enter a joint **Panel ElasticNet** ($\ell_1\text{ ratio} = 0.85$) over a 12-point logarithmic penalty grid:
1. **Municipality-grouped 5-fold CV & one-step additive validation projection:** Municipalities are partitioned deterministically (`seed = 42`) into 5 disjoint cluster folds ($G_{\text{tr}}$ vs. $G_{\text{val}}$). Inside each fold:
   - **Training fold:** The outcome $y^{\text{tr}}$ is two-way demeaned via 20-step alternating projections ($\tilde{y}^{\text{tr}}$, **unscaled** by outcome SD), while each predictor column $x_j^{\text{tr}}$ is two-way demeaned via 20-step alternating projections ($\tilde{x}_j^{\text{tr}}$) and standardised using its training mean and population SD $(\mu_j^{\text{tr}}, \sigma_j^{\text{tr}})$. Direct verification across all $5{,}310$ training-fold series ($9\text{ runs} \times 5\text{ folds} \times 118\text{ variables}$) comparing the 20-step operational CV solution against a more tightly converged reference solution (tolerance $10^{-14}$; production main-estimation tolerance is $10^{-10}$) confirms a maximum discrepancy of $\max |\tilde{v}^{(20)} - \tilde{v}^{(\text{ref})}| = 1.12 \times 10^{-11}$ ($2.30 \times 10^{-10}$ in SD-scaled units).
   - **One-step additive validation projection:** Validation rows use a one-step estimate of training time effects from entity-centred training data for any series $w \in \{y, x_1, \dots, x_P\}$:
     $$\bar{w}_i^{\text{tr}} = \frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_i} w_{it}\;\;(i \in G_{\text{tr}}), \qquad \hat{\lambda}_{w,t}^{\text{tr}} = \frac{1}{|G_{\text{tr}}(t)|}\sum_{i \in G_{\text{tr}}(t)} \left(w_{it} - \bar{w}_i^{\text{tr}}\right)$$
     For held-out validation municipalities $i \in G_{\text{val}}$, their **full validation time-series mean** $\bar{w}_i^{\text{val}} = \frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_i} w_{it}$ is subtracted alongside $\hat{\lambda}_{w,t}^{\text{tr}}$, with predictors (and **not** $y$) scaled by training $(\mu_j^{\text{tr}}, \sigma_j^{\text{tr}})$:
     $$y_{it}^{\text{val}} = y_{it} - \bar{y}_i^{\text{val}} - \hat{\lambda}_{y,t}^{\text{tr}}, \qquad \tilde{x}_{it,j}^{\text{val}} = \frac{\left(x_{it,j} - \bar{x}_{i,j}^{\text{val}} - \hat{\lambda}_{x_j,t}^{\text{tr}}\right) - \mu_j^{\text{tr}}}{\sigma_j^{\text{tr}}}$$
     Out-of-fold validation MSE $\frac{1}{N_{\text{val}}}\sum (y_{it}^{\text{val}} - \tilde{x}_{it}^{\text{val}\top}\hat{\beta}^{\text{tr}})^2$ selects $\alpha^*$.
   - **Validation-design limitations & 60-step sensitivity check:** Two properties of this CV scheme must be distinguished:
     1. *Conditional within-municipality scope:* Subtracting $\bar{y}_i^{\text{val}}$ uses each held-out municipality's complete observed outcome time series to remove its baseline level $\alpha_i$; CV therefore evaluates **within-municipality slope generalisation conditional on known municipal levels**, not cold-start level prediction for an unobserved municipality.
     2. *One-step vs. converged training time effects:* On unbalanced training folds, the one-step estimate $\hat{\lambda}_{w,t}^{\text{tr}}$ does not solve the joint two-way normal equations on $G_{\text{tr}}$. As a sensitivity check, 60 alternating-projection iterations (tolerance $10^{-10}$) were run **exclusively on the training-fold observations** $(i \in G_{\text{tr}})$ — using **zero validation observations** to estimate the converged training time effects $\hat{\lambda}_{w,t}^{\text{tr,(60)}}$. Those training-derived time effects were then subtracted from held-out validation rows alongside validation-municipality means $\hat{\alpha}_{w,i}^{\text{val}} = \frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_i}(w_{it} - \hat{\lambda}_{w,t}^{\text{tr,(60)}})$ computed from each validation municipality's full validation time series (note that even with converged training time effects, this out-of-fold transformation is a hybrid training-time / validation-entity demeaning step rather than a joint two-way projection on the validation fold itself). Using $\hat{\lambda}_{w,t}^{\text{tr,(60)}}$ selects a larger penalty $\alpha^*$ ($1.5\times$ to $3\times$ higher in 7 of 9 segments; identical in 2); feeding those penalties into Stability Selection leaves the Stage 3 covariate set **100% unchanged in `Houses / ALL`**, preserves the **exact same 7 indicators in `Apartments / ALL`** (shifting `rnal_density_per_km2` from lag $t-1$ to $t$), and in `Total / ALL` shifts `rnal_density_per_km2` from $t-1$ to $t$, swaps the population scale alias (`pop_residente` $\to$ `pop_densidade_km2`), and replaces one non-significant covariate (`empresas_nao_financeiras` $\to$ `medicos_por_1k_hab`).
   - **Data-construction isolation:** Fold-specific demeaning and standardisation do not, by themselves, guarantee complete isolation of the cross-validation preprocessing pipeline. Two upstream steps execute before municipality-grouped CV folds and Stability Selection subsamples are formed:
     - *Layer 1 imputation:* Executed once globally across all 35 municipalities during master-panel construction, so held-out municipalities can contribute to the cross-sectional median increments used to impute missing training-municipality cells. Direct audit across all 29 Stage 1 eligible municipal predictors ($29 \times 35 \times 18 = 18{,}270$ municipality-year cells) shows that cross-sectional median or increment imputation affects **195 cells (`1.07%`)**, confined strictly to **3 series** (`dormidas_turisticas_per_1k_hab`: 12 initial-year median + 83 increment cells; `capacidade_alojamento_turistico_per_1k_hab`: 3 + 80 cells; `renda_mediana_m2`: 0 + 17 cells), while the remaining 26 eligible municipal predictors receive only zero-increment municipality-specific carry-forwards (`1,435` end-of-sample cells).
     - *Layer 2 imputation:* Computed during design-matrix assembly prior to CV fold and Stability Selection separation. Municipality-specific means $\bar{x}_i$ use only municipality $i$'s own observations and therefore do not leak across municipalities under municipality-grouped folds or subsamples (while using each held-out validation municipality's full observed time series, consistent with the conditional-within-municipality CV scope above). By contrast, the global fallback mean $\bar{x}$ (used when a municipality has zero non-null values in a `(predictor, lag)` column) pools across all municipalities in the active sample before splitting; direct audit across all 9 Stage 2 discovery runs shows `0` global-fallback cells in 7 runs and `6` cells each in `Houses · ALL` and `Houses · AML`.
     Consequently, the grouped CV and Stability Selection procedures isolate **estimation-time demeaning and standardisation**, not upstream Layer 1 cross-sectional increments or Layer 2 global-fallback fills; the selected penalty $\alpha^*$ and selection frequencies $\hat{\pi}_{k,l}$ are conditional on those pre-split inputs. Reconstructing both Layer 1 increments and any Layer 2 global-fallback means strictly within each CV fold and subsample is noted as a planned refinement in Section 7.
2. **Stability Selection:** Across $B = 100$ cluster subsamples of size $\text{round}(0.70\,G)$ municipalities drawn without replacement at penalty $\alpha_{\text{stab}} = 0.70\,\alpha^*$, empirical selection frequency is recorded as $\hat{\pi}_{k,l} = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \mathbb{I}(|\hat{\beta}^{(b)}_{k,l}| > 10^{-4})$.
   - **Error-control note:** Unlike formal Meinshausen–Bühlmann (2010) thresholding, **no hard probability cut-off $\pi_{\text{thr}}$ or analytical per-family error bound $\mathbb{E}[V]$ is imposed**; $\hat{\pi}_{k,l}$ serves as a continuous stability weight in Stage 3 ranking, while multiplicity control relies on Benjamini–Hochberg FDR in Stage 2B.

### 3.3 Univariate TWFE Screening & Benjamini–Hochberg FDR (Stage 2B)
Stage 2 discovery (`Stage 2A` and `Stage 2B`) is executed **solely against real bank appraisals (`log_real_appraisal`, `2011–2026`)**, which provide the longest and deepest municipal panel across all three dwelling typologies. Each of the 117 `(predictor, lag)` candidates is estimated individually on non-null master-panel cells via standardised TWFE OLS with municipality-clustered (CR1) standard errors. Any candidate failing minimum estimability guards ($< 30$ non-null observations, $G_{k,l} < 10$ represented municipalities, $< 3$ distinct years, or two-way demeaned SD $< 10^{-9}$) is excluded prior to multiplicity adjustment, setting the Benjamini–Hochberg family size to the number of estimable candidates $m_{\text{est}} \le 117$; empirically, **all 117 candidates pass estimability in every one of the nine appraisal families ($m_{\text{est}} = 117$ throughout; $9 \times 117 = 1{,}053$ estimated regressions)**. Each candidate regression computes cluster degrees of freedom **$G_{k,l} - 1$** from the actual number of municipalities $G_{k,l}$ present in that candidate's non-null subsample (rather than defaulting to the scope-wide $G$). Raw two-sided Student-$t$ $p$-values ($G_{k,l} - 1$ d.f.) are adjusted within each `(dwelling_type, metro_scope)` family via **Benjamini–Hochberg** step-up $q$-values (`fdr_q_value < 0.05`). The Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment is applied separately within each of the nine appraisal discovery families; it does not provide joint FDR control across all nine appraisal families simultaneously ($1{,}053$ tests) or across the inherited transaction-model specifications.

## 4. Parsimonious TWFE Estimation & Variance Allocation (Stage 3)

### 4.1 Specification Selection Heuristic, National Series Role & Inherited Sales Discovery
The Stage 3 greedy municipal selection pool is drawn exclusively from the **29 Stage 1 eligible municipal predictors**; the **10 national `BPstat`/Eurosystem series** are treated separately as density-interacted exposures, with only the contemporaneous implicit mortgage interest-rate interaction retained in the baseline estimating equation.

Specifically, for each `(dwelling_type, metro_scope, target_metric)`, the 29 municipal candidates (`BPstat` excluded) are collapsed to their single highest-scoring lag using the Stage 2 appraisal-discovery metrics:
$$\text{Score}_{k,l} = \mathbb{I}(q_{k,l} < 0.05) + 0.55\,\hat{\pi}_{k,l} + 0.45\,\min\!\left(1,\, \frac{|t_{k,l}|}{6}\right)$$
Predictors are then added greedily in descending score order up to **7 municipal covariates**, evaluated on the **exact sample of the target metric being estimated** (`2011–2026` for bank appraisals; `2019–2026` for effective dwelling sales), subject to:
1. Non-null master-panel share $\ge 60\%$ over the target estimation window;
2. Alias exclusion (`pop_residente` vs. `pop_densidade_km2`; `rnal_density_per_1k_hab` vs. `rnal_density_per_km2`);
3. Pairwise two-way demeaned correlation $|r| \le 0.80$ with already selected covariates on the target sample;
4. Maximum $\text{VIF} \le 10.0$ on the target sample once 4 or more covariates are active.

**Role of the 10 national `BPstat` series across Stages 2 and 3:** Although all 10 national macroeconomic, credit, and regulatory series enter the 117-column Stage 2A/2B candidate catalogue after their baseline-density interactions $z_{i,\mathcal{G}} \cdot (M_{t-l} - \bar{M}_{\mathcal{G}})$ and 3-lag expansion ($10 \times 3 = 30$ national-interaction columns alongside $29 \times 3 = 87$ municipal columns, receiving selection frequencies, univariate $t$-statistics, and BH $q$-values in the discovery ranking), they are excluded from greedy covariate selection in Stage 3. Instead, the contemporaneous implicit mortgage interest-rate interaction — `metro_core_x_rate_shock` $= z_{i,\mathcal{G}} \cdot (r_t - \bar{r}_{\mathcal{G}})$ at lag $0$ — is **specified *ex ante*** in all 12 Stage 3 specifications as a common control for differential interest-rate exposure across municipalities. The remaining nine national series (and non-zero lags of $r_t$) therefore inform the Stage 2 discovery audit without competing for inclusion in the final Stage 3 estimating equations.

**Inherited discovery in Effective Sales models:** The three `Effective Sales` models (`Total × {ALL, AMP, AML}`) **do not have their own independent Stage 2A Stability Selection frequencies ($\hat{\pi}$) or Stage 2B FDR-adjusted screening results ($q$-values and univariate $t$-statistics)**; their candidate composite scores ($\text{Score}_{k,l}$) are inherited directly from the corresponding `Total` bank-appraisal discovery run (`ALL`, `AMP`, or `AML`), while Stage 3 coverage, collinearity, and VIF filters are evaluated on the `2019–2026` sales sample. Re-running Stage 2 discovery directly on the short 6–7 year sales series (tested in Section 5.5) yields substantially different candidate rankings.

### 4.2 Estimating Equation & Fixed-Effect Rank Connectivity
$$\ln(P^{\text{real}}_{it,s}) = \alpha_{i,s} + \lambda_{t,s} + \sum_{k \in \mathcal{S}^*_s} \beta_{k,s}\, X^{(k)}_{i,t-l_k} + \gamma_s \left[z_{i,\mathcal{G}} \cdot \left(r_t - \bar{r}_{\mathcal{G}}\right)\right] + \varepsilon_{it,s}$$
Baseline covariance estimation uses municipality-clustered **CR1**:
$$\widehat{V}_{\text{CR1}}(\hat{\beta}) = \frac{G}{G-1}\frac{N-1}{N - K_{\text{abs}}}\,(\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X})^{-1}\left(\sum_{g=1}^G \tilde{X}_g^\top \hat{e}_g \hat{e}_g^\top \tilde{X}_g\right)(\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X})^{-1}$$
tested against $t_{G-1}$. Because the fixed-effect rank expression $G + (T - 1)$ inside $K_{\text{abs}} = K + G + (T - 1)$ requires a connected bipartite municipality–year observation graph on unbalanced panels, breadth-first connectivity checks were run on the municipality–year incidence graph of **all 12 Stage 3 estimation samples**, confirming **exactly 1 connected component** in every specification (so $N - K_{\text{abs}}$ matches the exact residual degrees of freedom throughout).

### 4.3 Shapley/LMG Variance Allocation & Absolute Linear Contribution
1. **Shapley / Lindeman–Merenda–Gold (LMG) $R^2_{\text{within}}$ Decomposition:**
   - Partitioning within-municipality explained variance across correlated regressors applies **Shapley/LMG** averaging over all $2^K = 256$ subsets of $\tilde{X}$ (Shapley, 1953; Lindeman et al., 1980; Lipovetsky & Conklin, 2001):
     $$\text{LMG}_j = \sum_{S \subseteq \{1,\dots,K\}\setminus\{j\}} \frac{|S|!\,(K - |S| - 1)!}{K!}\left[R^2_{\text{within}}(S \cup \{j\}) - R^2_{\text{within}}(S)\right]$$
     where exact OLS subset regressions satisfy $\sum_{j=1}^K \text{LMG}_j = R^2_{\text{within}}$ and analytical non-negativity $R^2_{\text{within}}(S \cup \{j\}) \ge R^2_{\text{within}}(S)$ on identical observations, with relative shares $\text{LMG}\%_j = 100 \times \text{LMG}_j / R^2_{\text{within}}$. In the frozen baseline pipeline, each subset regression is solved via the pure-Python linear solver with a $10^{-9}$ diagonal ridge stabiliser (`ridge = 1e-9`), making the published values **numerically regularised Shapley/LMG allocations** rather than strictly unregularised OLS subset fits. Direct verification using an unregularised Householder QR least-squares solver across all $12 \times 256 = 3{,}072$ subsets confirms that every subset is full rank, every nested increment is non-negative, exact OLS closure holds to $\le 1.7 \times 10^{-16}$, and published shares agree with unregularised OLS Shapley/LMG shares to within $\le 8.2 \times 10^{-7}$ percentage points (Section 5.1).
   - **Interpretation:** Shapley/LMG shares are **in-sample, specification-conditional variance allocations**, **not** causal effect shares or policy counterfactuals.
2. **Absolute Linear Contribution / Linear Effect Attribution:**
   - Global relative effect magnitudes are computed as $\overline{|\phi_j|} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N |\hat{\beta}_j \tilde{x}_{nj}|$ (normalised to $100\%$), and local municipality-year pillar shares normalise $|\hat{\beta}_j \tilde{x}_{it,j}| + 10^{-4}\overline{|\phi_j|}$ across five thematic pillars, where $10^{-4}\overline{|\phi_j|}$ is purely a numerical stabilisation term preventing zero-denominator division when all demeaned covariates lie near zero rather than a substantive contribution.
3. **Note on Dashboard UI Labelling Inaccuracies:**
   - In the pre-compiled interactive dashboard (`dist/index.html`) and static chart headers, these two metrics are labelled `"Owen/LMG"` and `"SHAP"`. Both UI labels are **terminologically imprecise**: (i) no *a priori* coalition structure (Owen, 1977) is used during estimation — individual Shapley/LMG values are computed per regressor and summed *ex post* into thematic pillars; and (ii) the second metric computes **direct absolute linear contributions** ($|\hat{\beta}_j \tilde{x}_{it,j}|$), not conditional-expectation Shapley (SHAP) values accounting for regressor correlation. Updating these UI headers in the compiled bundle is listed in Section 7.

### 5.2 Subsample-Isolated Preprocessing & Training-Anchored Penalty Grid in Stage 2A
- **Audit finding (CV vs. Stability Selection):** Grouped cross-validation and Stability Selection are distinct resampling stages in Stage 2A. In the baseline pipeline, while the 5-fold grouped CV loop recomputes two-way demeaning and standardisation inside each training fold, `alpha_max` anchoring of the 12-point penalty grid uses the full sample, and the **Stability Selection** loop reuses per-municipality Gram matrices constructed from full-sample two-way demeaned and standardised series rather than recomputing demeaning and standardisation inside each 70%-municipality subsample.
- **Exact penalty calibration and preprocessing in the isolated audit:** To separate the two resampling stages explicitly, the alternative procedure modifies Stage 2A as follows (conditional on the upstream full-panel Layer 1 master panel; see Section 3.2):
  1. **Grouped 5-fold CV ($\alpha^*$ selection):** Inside each training fold $f \in \{1, \dots, 5\}$, two-way demeaning and standardisation are computed on the training municipalities $G_{\text{tr}}^{(f)}$, yielding a training-fold maximum correlation penalty $\alpha_{\max,f}^{\text{tr}} = \max(10^{-4},\, \max_j |(\tilde{x}_j^{\text{tr}})^\top \tilde{y}^{\text{tr}}| / (N_{\text{tr}}^{(f)}\cdot \rho_{\ell_1}))$. The 12-point logarithmic penalty grid is anchored at the **mean of the five training-fold maxima**, $\bar{\alpha}_{\max}^{\text{tr}} = \frac{1}{5}\sum_{f=1}^5 \alpha_{\max,f}^{\text{tr}}$ (rather than full-sample $\alpha_{\max}$), and out-of-fold validation MSE selects $\alpha^*$ from this grid.
  2. **Stability Selection ($\alpha_{\text{stab}}$ and subsample-specific preprocessing):** The Stability Selection penalty is then **held fixed across all $B = 100$ cluster subsamples at $\alpha_{\text{stab}} = 0.70\,\alpha^*$** (derived directly from the CV-selected $\alpha^*$; no subsample-specific $\alpha_{\max,b}$ is recalculated inside each subsample $b$). Inside every $70\%$-municipality subsample $b$, two-way demeaning (20 alternating-projection iterations) and predictor standardisation $(\mu_j^{(b)}, \sigma_j^{(b)})$ are recomputed **exclusively on the drawn $70\%$ municipalities** prior to coordinate descent at $\alpha_{\text{stab}}$.
- **Audit results across all 9 discovery runs propagated to all 12 Stage 3 specifications:** Re-running all nine `(dwelling_type, metro_scope)` appraisal discovery runs under this procedure yields selection probabilities $\hat{\pi}_{k,l}$ correlated at **$r \in [0.9805,\, 0.9954]$** with baseline values (mean $|\Delta\hat{\pi}| \in [0.015,\, 0.048]$). Propagating those rankings into all **12** Stage 3 specifications establishes:
  - **7 of 12 models keep identical covariate sets** (exact same 7 municipal predictors, lags, and transformations, yielding algebraically identical estimates, $R^2_{\text{within}}$, and LMG shares): **all three aggregate (`ALL`) bank-appraisal models** (`Total`, `Apartments`, `Houses`), `Appraisals · Total · AMP`, `Appraisals · Apartments · AML`, `Sales · Total · AMP`, and `Sales · Total · AML`.
  - **5 of 12 models swap exactly one municipal covariate**, while direct verification confirms that the **other six municipal predictors, their exact lags, and their functional/stationarity transformations remain identical** (alongside the fixed `metro_core_x_rate_shock` control), and none of the newly entering covariates are individually significant under CR2 or WCB-Webb ($B = 1{,}999$):
    1. `Appraisals · Houses · AMP`: swaps a constant-area population scale alias (`pop_residente` ($t$) $\to$ `pop_densidade_km2` ($t$), $p_{\text{CR2}} = 0.4455$, $p_{\text{WCB-Webb}} = 0.3210$), leaving $R^2_{\text{within}}$ (`0.4826` $\to$ `0.4827`) and `AL spatial density` ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{std}} = +0.550$, $p_{\text{CR2}} = 0.0215$, $p_{\text{WCB-Webb}} = 0.0195$, LMG `46.2%`) virtually unchanged;

Promotion of the subsample-isolated procedure to the production baseline, followed by regeneration of the affected model estimates, inference, and dashboard outputs, is planned for the next release (Section 7).

3. **Interpreting $R^2_{\text{within}}$ differences across windows:** Higher $R^2_{\text{within}}$ in `M1` and `M2/M3` relative to `M0` **does not imply superior model specification**; changing the estimation horizon alters total within-sample variance $\sum \tilde{y}_{it}^2$, removes projected `2025–2026` covariate cells (where constant carry-forward introduces time-localised end-of-sample measurement error), and excludes the `2023–2026` monetary-tightening and post-pandemic regime.
4. **In-window stability (`M3` vs. `M4`):** Holding the outcome sample fixed at `2011–2022`, both the slope and conditional LMG allocation of `rnal_density_per_km2` remain stable across fixed (`M3`) and re-selected (`M4`) specifications (`22.4` vs. `22.5` p.p. in `Total`; `16.8` vs. `16.9` p.p. in `Houses`; `22.7` vs. `19.5` p.p. in `Apartments`), while `rnal_new_reg_total` in `Apartments` loses significance once `2025–2026` is dropped ($p_{\text{WCB-Webb}} = 0.0215 \to 0.1125$).

## 6. Scope Conditions & Limitations

1. **Conditional associations & variance allocations (non-causal):** Neither TWFE regression slopes nor Shapley/LMG within-$R^2$ shares identify causal effects. TWFE coefficients capture conditional within-municipality co-movements net of additive municipality and year fixed effects (leaving potential time-varying confounding unaddressed), and Shapley/LMG shares represent descriptive, specification-dependent decompositions of in-sample explained variance.
2. **Post-selection inference:** Because Stage 2 screening and Stage 3 estimation are performed on the same historical panel, Stage 3 standard errors, confidence intervals, and $p$-values reflect precision conditional on the selected model rather than post-selection-adjusted coverage.
3. **Small-cluster leverage vs. genuine metropolitan heterogeneity:** In `AMP` ($G \le 17$) and `AML` ($G \le 18$), asymptotic CR1 standard errors can be anticonservative when predictor variance is concentrated in one or two urban cores. Following Bell & McCaffrey (2002) and Imbens & Kolesár (2016), Satterthwaite degrees-of-freedom values below approximately `4`–`5` indicate very low effective degrees of freedom (a diagnostic convention signalling high single-cluster leverage where asymptotic normal/$t_{G-1}$ approximations are unreliable); exact `df_BM` values are therefore reported alongside regional estimates in Section 5.3. Metropolitan-specific findings need **not** appear in the pooled `ALL` panel to be credible — regional associations that pass CR2 and Wild Cluster Bootstrap with adequate effective degrees of freedom (`df_BM = 6.5–10.8`, such as foreign resident growth and new AL registrations in `AML` sales, formal employment in `AML` appraisals, or completed dwellings in `AMP` appraisals) represent well-supported metropolitan heterogeneity, whereas estimates with very low `df_BM` that fail CR2/WCB (such as rehabilitation completions in `AML` houses, `df_BM = 2.1`) are treated as unconfirmed.
4. **Distinct underlying property universes:** Bank appraisals cover mortgage-financed valuations whereas transaction deeds cover all registered sales (including cash purchases) on potentially different dwelling compositions; comparisons between the two series are descriptive and do not measure valuation gaps on matched properties.

## 7. Planned Methodological Improvements (Future Releases)

While the current public bundle (`dist/index.html` and static charts) is frozen for reproducibility, the methodological audit above identifies seven concrete pipeline refinements planned for future releases:

1. **Subsample-isolated Stability Selection & fold-isolated Layer 1/Layer 2 imputation as the default production baseline:** Replace full-sample Gram-matrix reuse and full-sample `alpha_max` grid anchoring in Stage 2A with the two-stage procedure documented in Section 5.2 (CV penalty grid anchored at the mean of the five training-fold maxima $\bar{\alpha}_{\max}^{\text{tr}}$ to select $\alpha^*$, followed by subsample-specific two-way demeaning and standardisation inside every 70% municipality subsample at fixed $\alpha_{\text{stab}} = 0.70\,\alpha^*$), extend isolation upstream by reconstructing Layer 1 cross-sectional median increments and any Layer 2 global-fallback means strictly within each CV fold and Stability Selection subsample, and regenerate all affected model estimates, inference, and dashboard outputs.
2. **Unregularised QR/SVD subset solver for exact Shapley/LMG & $\ln(x)$ for monetary predictors:** Replace the $10^{-9}$ ridge-stabilised subset linear solver in Stage 3 with an unregularised Householder QR or SVD least-squares solver (yielding exact OLS Shapley/LMG closure to floating-point machine tolerance without regularisation), and migrate strictly positive monetary predictors (`ganho_medio_mensal`, `irs_rendimento_bruto_medio`, `new_housing_credit_eur_m`, `despesa_capital_municipal_per_1k_hab`, and `renda_mediana_m2`) from unit-dependent $\ln(1 + x)$ to $\ln(x)$.
3. **Global 35-municipality reference scale for baseline urban density ($z_i$):** Standardise $\ln(\text{Pop}_{i,2009}/\text{Area}_i)$ once across all 35 metropolitan municipalities rather than separately within each estimation scope (`ALL`, `AMP`, `AML`), placing unstandardised interest-rate exposure slopes ($\hat{\gamma}_s$) on a common unit scale across geographic scopes while leaving within-standardised effects, $p$-values, and LMG shares unchanged.
4. **Unified annual-average index-level price deflator:** Replace the hybrid backward-chained rate deflator (`2010–2024` mean monthly HICP YoY rates spliced onto `2025` national CPI and provisional `2026` carry-forward) with a single consistent annual-average HICP (`ECB ICP.M.PT.N.000000.4.INX`) or national INE CPI index-level series — an update that can be applied independently for historical years (`2009–2025`) without waiting for `Q4 2026` housing releases.
5. **Full-year `2026` data finalisation:** Once complete `Q4 2026` housing outcomes, full-year `2026` RNAL and monthly interest-rate series, and published `2026` annual inflation are released, replace all provisional partial-year `2026` entries and the provisional `2026` deflator carry-forward assumption.
